# How to Highlight and Leave Sticky Notes on Any Website

To highlight text and leave sticky notes on any website, use a Chrome extension that saves your marks with the page and puts them back when you return. In Pagic, open **Markup**, pick the highlighter or a sticky note, and mark up the page; next time you visit, your highlights are on the same words and your notes are where you left them. Here's how it works, and how it compares to research highlighters.

## Two kinds of web highlighters

There are two different ideas behind "highlight the web":

1. **Collect highlights.** Tools like Web Highlights, Glasp and Weava save what you highlight into a library or dashboard, so you can search and export quotes across hundreds of pages. Great for research and citations.
2. **Mark up the page itself.** The highlights, notes and drawings live on the page, like ink in a book. When you come back, the page looks the way you left it. That's what Pagic's Markup does.

If you need a searchable library of quotes, pick a collector. If you want to open a recipe, a doc page or an article and see your own notes on it, pick the second kind.

## How to highlight text on a web page with Pagic

1. Open the page, click Pagic's icon and **Customize this site**.
2. Click **Markup**. A toolbar appears at the bottom.
3. Choose **Highlight** and drag across words. They light up letter by letter, in yellow, green, blue, pink, purple or red.
4. Above the toolbar, switch to an **underline** (plain, double, squiggly or dotted) or a **strikethrough** if you prefer.

A highlight belongs to its words, not to a spot on the screen. If the page reflows, zooms or the site moves that paragraph, the highlight stays on the same words. Pagic saves the words with a little of the text around them, so it finds them again on the next visit.

## How to put a sticky note on a website

Pick **Note** in Markup. A sticky note follows your pointer and outlines what it would stick to; click to place it and start typing. Notes are pinned to that part of the page, so they move with it when you scroll or the page changes layout, and hide when it's not on the page.

You can change a note's color, font and size from its **⋯** menu, drag it onto another element, or resize it. The Settings page has note styles too: Chat bubble, Sticker, Stitched patch, Classic, Taped paper or Ruled card.

## Draw, point and black out

Markup has more than highlighting:

- **Pen** in seven colors and three sizes, plus **arrows, lines, rectangles and ellipses**, in a sketchy, clean or scribbled style.
- **Black marker** to black out words like a classified document, for screenshots you're about to share.
- **Spotlight** dims everything except the part you drag around, and **Laser** draws strokes that fade away. Both are for screen sharing.

## Are my notes private?

Yes. Pagic has no account and no server for your notes. Highlights, notes and drawings are saved in your browser and only you see them. With Pro, you can export a site's preset to a file, notes included, and share it with someone on purpose.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I highlight text on a website and save it?** Yes. With Pagic's Markup, highlights are saved per site and shown on the same words every time you visit.
**How do I add sticky notes to a web page?** Open Pagic's Markup, choose Note, click where it should stick and type. It stays on that spot of the page on later visits.
**Do highlights survive if the website changes?** Pagic saves the highlighted words with their surrounding text and finds them again even if the site moves them. If the words themselves are removed, the highlight can't come back.
**Can others see my highlights?** No. They're stored in your browser. Sharing is only by exporting a preset file yourself, which is part of Pro.
**What's the difference between Pagic and Glasp or Web Highlights?** Those collect highlights into a library you can search across pages. Pagic keeps highlights and notes on the page itself, along with drawings, hiding and colors.
