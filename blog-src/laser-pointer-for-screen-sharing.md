# How to Use a Laser Pointer and Spotlight While Screen Sharing

To point at things on a web page while screen sharing, use a browser tool that draws on top of the page, since a mouse cursor is hard to follow on a call. Pagic's **Markup** has a **laser** whose strokes fade a moment after you draw them, a **spotlight** that dims everything except the part you drag around, and arrows and highlights that stay. They work in Chrome while you share a tab or window on Zoom, Google Meet or Teams.

## Why a cursor isn't enough

On the other end of a screen share, your cursor is small, it moves too fast, and video compression smudges it. "Up here, no, the other button" is how half of every demo goes. A laser stroke or a dimmed page is impossible to miss.

## The laser pointer

1. Open the page you're presenting and click Pagic's icon.
2. Open **Markup** and pick **Laser**.
3. Draw on the page. The glowing stroke fades away a moment after you lift the pointer, and nothing is saved.

Circle a number, underline a sentence, draw an arrow to a button. Then keep talking; it cleans up after itself.

## The spotlight

Pick **Spotlight** and drag around the part of the page you're talking about. Everything else dims. Make as many as you like, to show two things at once.

Spotlights are saved and stick to what they were made on, so the page stays dimmed around them until you remove them: point at one and click its trash button, or switch it off in **Changes**. Handy for a page you present every week.

## Arrows, highlights and notes that stay

For pages you'll share again, use the parts of Markup that are saved:

- **Highlight** words in six colors, or underline them.
- **Arrows, rectangles and ellipses**, in a sketchy whiteboard style or clean lines. Hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> for straight lines and perfect circles.
- **Sticky notes** with talking points, pinned to the part of the page they're about.
- **Black marker** to black out a name or a number before you share.

Next time you open the page, everything's where you left it. More in [highlights and sticky notes on any website](/blog/highlight-and-sticky-notes-on-websites/).

## Hide what you don't want on screen

Before a call, hide the parts of a page your audience shouldn't see, like a sidebar with other customers' names, with [Edit page → Hide](/blog/hide-elements-on-any-website/), or blur them with **Style → Blur**. It's all undoable after the call.

## Does this work in Zoom, Meet and Teams?

Yes. Pagic draws on the web page itself, so whatever you share (a Chrome tab, the window or the whole screen) shows the laser, spotlight and marks. It doesn't draw over other apps, only over web pages in your browser.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I get a laser pointer when screen sharing?** Use an on-page tool. Pagic's Markup has a Laser whose strokes fade after a moment, and it shows in any screen share of the browser.
**Can I highlight part of the screen during a Zoom call?** On web pages, yes. Pagic's Spotlight dims everything except the area you drag around, and Highlight marks text.
**Do the laser strokes get saved?** No. Laser strokes fade and aren't saved. Highlights, arrows, notes and spotlights are saved until you remove them.
**Does it work on slides?** It works on web pages, including web-based slides in the browser. It doesn't draw over desktop apps like Keynote or PowerPoint.
**Can people on the call see my notes?** Only if they're on the page you're sharing. Your notes are stored in your browser and aren't sent anywhere.
