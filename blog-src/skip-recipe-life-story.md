# How to Skip the Life Story on Recipe Sites

To skip the life story on a recipe site, press the site's **Jump to Recipe** button if it has one, or open the page in a reader mode, which strips the ads, pop-ups and most of the essay. Pagic's Reader opens any page as a clean article with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>, free on every site, and Pagic's sticky notes let you write "half the sugar" right on the recipe for next time.

## Why are recipe pages so long?

Mostly ads and search. Longer pages hold more ad slots, and personal stories and tips give search engines more text to match. Many recipe writers genuinely like telling the story too. None of that helps when you're standing in the kitchen with flour on your hands.

## Four ways to get to the recipe

### 1. Jump to Recipe

Many recipe sites have a **Jump to Recipe** button near the top. It scrolls straight to the recipe card. Quick, but you still get the ads and pop-ups around the card.

### 2. The print view

The recipe card often has a **Print** button that opens a plain page with only the ingredients and steps. It's one of the cleanest views there is, and you don't have to actually print it.

### 3. Chrome's reading mode

Right-click and choose **Open in reading mode**. Chrome shows the text without most of the page around it. On recipe sites it sometimes keeps the story and drops the recipe card, depending on how the page is built.

### 4. Pagic's Reader

Press <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd> (<kbd>⌥⇧R</kbd> on Mac). Reader shows the title, the pictures and the text on a calm page in your font, size and theme, without the ads, sidebars and pop-ups. Long pages get an **On this page** outline, so you can jump to the ingredients in one click. It's free on every site.

## Keep your changes to the recipe

Recipes you cook more than once get tweaks: less salt, a different pan, ten more minutes. With Pagic's **Markup**, stick a note right next to the ingredient, or highlight the step you always forget. Notes are pinned to that part of the page and are there the next time you open it. More in [highlighting and sticky notes on any website](/blog/highlight-and-sticky-notes-on-websites/).

If it's a recipe site you use all the time, you can also hide its pop-ups and newsletter boxes for good with [Edit page → Hide](/blog/hide-elements-on-any-website/).

## Which one should you use?

- **The site has a print view:** use it, it's the cleanest.
- **You read recipes on many different sites:** a reader mode with a shortcut, like Pagic's Reader.
- **You keep coming back to the same recipe:** sticky notes, so your tweaks are on the page.
