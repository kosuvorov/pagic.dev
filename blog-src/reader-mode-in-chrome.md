# Reader Mode in Chrome: Built-in vs Extensions (2026)

To use reader mode in Chrome, right-click a page and choose **Open in reading mode**, which since 2026 opens the article full screen without ads and sidebars (it used to live only in a side panel). For a reader view with a keyboard shortcut, Safari-style themes and your own fonts on every site, an extension like Pagic adds a Reader you open with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>. Here's how both work and when to use which.

## How to turn on Chrome's built-in reading mode

1. Open an article.
2. Right-click the page and choose **Open in reading mode**, or use the reading mode button if you've pinned it to the toolbar.
3. Chrome opens the text full screen, with options for font, size, spacing and colors, and **Read Aloud**.

Google announced this immersive reading mode for desktop Chrome in April 2026. Before that, reading mode only opened in the narrow side panel next to the page, and you can still switch back to the side panel if you prefer it.

**Good:** built in, free, and it can read pages aloud. **Not so good:** the reader view is Chrome's, so your choice of themes and fonts is Chrome's too, and it doesn't come with other page fixes.

## How Pagic's Reader works

Pagic's Reader is like Safari's Reader: it shows the article (title, author, text and pictures) on a clean page, without menus, ads or sidebars.

- **Open it with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>** (<kbd>⌥⇧R</kbd> on Mac) or from Pagic's panel. Esc closes it.
- **Themes:** Auto (white by day, night while your system is dark), White, Sepia, Gray or Night.
- **Fonts:** New York, Georgia, San Francisco, SF Mono, or any font on your computer, each shown in its own face.
- **Long articles** get an **On this page** outline, and wide tables scroll inside the column.
- **Forums and Q&A pages** keep the question and answers together, with the accepted answer first.
- **Reader filters out cookie notices** and hidden interface panels, and keeps code examples and expandable sections.

Reader is free on every site, and opening it doesn't give Pagic ongoing access to the site: it gets that one tab for the moment.

## When reader mode isn't the right fix

Reader mode is for articles. For a site you use, not just read (a feed, a dashboard, YouTube), reader mode has nothing to show. ## Frequently asked questions

**How do I enable reader mode in Chrome?** Right-click the page and choose Open in reading mode. Chrome opens the article full screen; you can switch it back to the side panel in its options.
**Does Chrome have a reader mode shortcut?** Not by default. Pagic's Reader opens with Alt+Shift+R, and you can change the shortcut at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
**Why doesn't reader mode work on some pages?** Reader mode needs an article to find. Home pages, feeds and app-like sites don't have one, and some pages show an access check instead of the article.
**Is Pagic's Reader free?** Yes, on every site, even on Pagic's free plan.
**Can reader mode remove cookie banners?** Pagic's Reader filters out cookie notices when it builds the clean page. It doesn't accept or reject them on the site.
