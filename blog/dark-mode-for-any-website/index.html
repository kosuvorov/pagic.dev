How to Get Dark Mode on Any Website in Chrome

To get dark mode on any website in Chrome, you have three options: Chrome's hidden "Auto Dark Mode for Web Contents" flag, which darkens every site with no settings; a dark mode extension like Dark Reader; or Pagic, which adds dark mode per site with an Auto mode that follows your system and keeps photos in their real colors. Here's how each works, and which one I'd pick for what.

Why doesn't Chrome's dark mode darken websites?

Chrome's own dark theme only changes Chrome: the tabs, the menus, the New Tab page. Websites decide their own colors. Sites that support dark mode read your system setting and switch. Plenty of sites never added it, so you get a dark browser around a glaring white page at night.

To darken those, something has to recolor the page for you.

Option 1: Chrome's dark mode flag

Chrome has an experimental setting that darkens every website.

  1. Type chrome://flags in the address bar.
  2. Search for Auto Dark Mode for Web Contents.
  3. Set it to Enabled and click Relaunch.

Good: free, built in, nothing to install. Not so good: it's all or nothing. There's no per-site switch, no brightness or contrast, and it applies to sites that already had a nice dark theme. Results vary a lot from site to site, and flags can change or disappear between Chrome versions.

Option 2: Dark Reader

Dark Reader is the best-known dark mode extension, open source and around for years. It generates a dark theme for every site, with sliders for brightness, contrast and sepia, and you can turn it on for all sites or only some.

Good: detailed control, works on most sites, a big community writing fixes for tricky ones. Not so good: it does one thing. If you also want to hide parts of a page, change fonts or recolor a site in something other than dark, that's more extensions.

Option 3: Pagic

Pagic's moon button turns on dark mode for the site you're on, as part of a bigger toolkit for making a site yours.

  • Click once for dark mode. Click again for Auto, which follows your system: dark only while your Mac or PC is in dark mode. If your system switches at sunset, so does the site.
  • Darkness softens it. At 100% white becomes black. Lower makes it a dark gray instead. It starts at 95%.
  • Photos and videos keep their real colors and contrast, at any Darkness. Pagic applies the exact opposite correction to images and videos, so a photo of a red car stays a red car.
  • Google Docs and Sheets get a real dark page. They draw their pages as pictures, which most dark mode tools either skip or turn into a negative, so Pagic handles them specially: the paper goes dark and the words go light.

It also reaches pop-up dialogs and fullscreen video, which the browser draws above the page.

If you want every site dark and nothing else, Dark Reader is a great pick. If the reason you want dark mode is "this site is hard on my eyes at night", it's usually not the only thing wrong with that site. That's where Pagic fits: dark mode, and the sidebar gone, and a bigger font, saved together.

How to set dark mode for just one site

In Pagic, everything you do is saved for the site you're on. So dark mode on Hacker News doesn't touch Wikipedia. I keep Hacker News dark all the time and Wikipedia light, in a different font.

If you want a site dark only at night, use Auto on the moon. With Pagic Pro you can go further: a preset can apply only while your system is dark, so a site can have a full "night" look (dark, bigger text, sidebar hidden) that takes over by itself when your system turns dark.

Can you make a site any color, not just dark?

Yes. Pagic's color pad tints the whole page to any hue: drag the big dot and whites become a pale shade of that color, blacks a deep one. It's how you get a warm sepia page for reading, or a mint one because you like mint. There's also greyscale, and with the big dot it becomes a one-color look in any hue.

Frequently asked questions

On LCD screens the difference is small. Why do photos look wrong with some dark mode extensions? Simple dark modes invert everything, including images.
